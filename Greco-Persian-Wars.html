THE PERSIAN WARS

The Greco-Persian Wars were a series of conflicts between the Achaemenid Empire and Greek city-states from 499 BC to 449 BC. It all began when Cyrus the Great conquered Ionia in 547 BC, leading to rebellions in Greece. The Ionian Revolt started in 499 BC but was crushed by Persia by 493 BC. Seeking revenge, Persia invaded Greece twice, with defeats at Marathon in 490 BC and Salamis in 480 BC. In 479 BC, the Greeks, led by Athens, decisively defeated the Persians at Plataea and Mycale, leading to Persian withdrawal from Europe. Following these victories, the Delian League, spearheaded by Athens, continued to campaign against Persia until around 451 BC, possibly marked by the Peace of Callias. This period also saw Greek involvement in other conflicts, such as the Egyptian revolt against Artaxerxes I from 460–454 BC. Despite setbacks, the Greeks maintained pressure on Persia, culminating in the eventual withdrawal of Persian garrisons from Europe and the restoration of independence to cities like Ionia.
