FIRST GREEK ALPHABET

The Greek alphabet originated from the Phoenician script around the 9th-8th centuries BC during early Archaic Greece. Originally, the Phoenician alphabet focused mainly on consonants, but over time, some vowels were indicated using matres lectionis. However, this system wasn't ideal for Greek, so adaptations were made to represent Greek vowels more consistently, though not always clearly. A Greek individual, familiar with Phoenician script, developed the Greek alphabet. It spread eastward to Phrygia and westward with traders to Euboea, where the Etruscans later adapted it, eventually leading to the Latin alphabet.
